Which Communication Framework?

STAR vs Pyramid vs Narrative Arc vs Executive Summary — which framework for which situation, and how they connect.

The Key Difference

These four frameworks all answer one question — how do I get my message across? — but for different channels. STAR tells a story out loud. Pyramid leads with the answer. Narrative Arc builds buy-in through tension. Executive Summary puts a decision on one page.

⭐
STAR Method
Situation → Task → Action → Result. Structure interview answers as 90-second stories.
BEST FOR: Behavioral interviews
🔺
Pyramid Principle
Answer first, then grouped reasons, then evidence. Top-down for efficiency.
BEST FOR: Email, Slack, exec updates
📖
Narrative Arc
Status Quo → Disruption → Struggle → Resolution → New Reality. Build buy-in through tension.
BEST FOR: Pitches & presentations
📋
Executive Summary
One-page decision doc: Recommendation, Context, Data, Options, Risks, Ask.
BEST FOR: Docs for leadership

One Sentence Each

STAR: "Here's a story that proves I can do this." Pyramid: "Here's my answer — and the three reasons behind it." Narrative Arc: "Feel the problem, then the solution lands." Exec Summary: "Everything you need to decide, on one page."

Quick Rules

Interview? → STAR (90s story with metrics)
Email / Slack? → Pyramid (answer first)
Presentation needing buy-in? → Narrative Arc (build tension, then resolve)
Document for leadership? → Executive Summary (1-page decision tool)
🎤
"Tell me about a time you led through conflict"
A behavioral interview question. You need a tight, memorable answer that proves the competency with evidence.
STAR Method
90-second story: set the Situation, your Task, the Action you took (50% of the answer), and a quantified Result.
📊
Presenting a roadmap pivot to a skeptical leadership team
They're attached to the current plan. You need them to feel why the status quo is failing before you reveal the new direction.
Narrative Arc
Status Quo → Disruption → Struggle (peak tension) → Resolution (the pivot) → New Reality. Land the proof at the resolution.
💬
Slack update: should we ship Friday or slip a week?
A quick async decision. People are scanning. Burying the answer wastes everyone's time.
Pyramid Principle
Lead with the recommendation: "Slip a week. Three reasons: QA gap, a P0 bug, and the holiday freeze." Detail underneath for anyone who wants it.
📋
One-page proposal for a new initiative going to the exec team
Leadership will read it cold, decide on it, and never see your slides. The page has to stand alone.
Executive Summary
Recommendation first, then Context, Key Data, Options, Risks, and the Ask. Written last, read first.
🎯
Big all-hands pitch plus a doc people take away
You're presenting live and need a leave-behind that survives without you in the room.
Narrative Arc + Exec Summary
Present with the Arc to win the room, then send the one-page Exec Summary so the decision sticks afterwards.

Power Combinations

The frameworks aren't rivals — they layer. The strongest communicators reach for two at once.

Presentation + follow-up: Present with Narrative Arc in the meeting, then send the Exec Summary as a follow-up doc. Spoken story + written one-pager = complete coverage.
Interview prep: Prepare 6-8 STAR stories. For case interviews, structure your analysis with Pyramid (conclusion first, grouped reasons).
Inside an Exec Summary: Use Pyramid structure for the Context and Key Data sections. The Exec Summary is a Pyramid in document form, with added Options and Risks.
Portfolio case studies: Write each project as a STAR story with an Exec Summary header. Recruiters scan the summary; interviewers hear the story.

For PM Interviews

You rarely need all four at once. But naming the right tool out loud signals communication maturity: "I'd answer this as a STAR story," or "Let me give you the headline first, Pyramid-style, then the reasoning." That framing alone reads as senior — it shows you choose your structure on purpose.
