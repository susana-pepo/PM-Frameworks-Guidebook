# Design Context — PM Frameworks Guidebook (v2)

## Users
Product managers and PM candidates learning, revisiting or interview-prepping
on 24 frameworks and 6 comparison guides. They read in focused sessions and
come back to look things up. The job: "teach me this framework well enough
that I can use it today", plus "help me choose between these tools".

## Direction
- **Product**: a study reference that teaches PMs 24 frameworks well enough to
  use them, plus guides for choosing between them.
- **Character**: calm, exact, quietly warm.
- **World**: a printed field guide. Paper, ink, ruled tables, numbered
  sections, thumb-index tabs on the page edge.
- **Moments**: each framework's own diagram (the hero figure) and its live
  worked numbers (calculator results). These carry the colour; chrome is quiet.
- **Signature**: the thumb index, a small category-coloured tab (`.thumb`).
  It is the only place a category colour enters the chrome.

## System (one source of truth)
- `src/styles/tokens.css`: every colour, size, space, radius, shadow and
  duration. Light and dark are both first-class; the OS preference is the
  default when the reader has not chosen.
- Type: **Literata** (display and reading prose) and **Hanken Grotesk**
  (interface, labels, tables, tools). No mono, no uppercase tracked labels,
  no italic for emphasis. Weights 400/500/600. Reading prose 18px/1.68 at a
  68ch measure; UI text 16px; the smallest text is 12px meta.
- Neutrals are warm paper and ink, nearly grey. Category colours appear as the
  thumb mark, the section label, the active contents number, and inside
  content figures. `data-series` 1–7 is the categorical palette for diagrams.
- Surfaces: hairline rules, `--paper-raised` cards, `--paper-sunk` wells.
  Shadows only on floating things (menus, palette) and very softly on tools.
  No gradients, glows, glass, grain, side-stripes or hover lifts.
- Motion: one strong ease-out (`--ease`, cubic-bezier(0.23, 1, 0.32, 1)),
  100/120/200ms, distances in tokens (`--rise` 4px, `--nudge` 3px, `--press`
  0.98). A new section fades and rises 4px (Web Animations, interruptible);
  menus and the phone contents sheet drop in, close faster than they open;
  a changed result settles once (not while dragging). Presses scale
  slightly; hovers only on fine pointers. Keyboard-opened search and the
  theme switch are instant. Reduced motion keeps fades, drops movement.
  Nothing loops.
- States: rows and links answer the pointer with their title (underline or
  colour) and an arrow nudge, never a box that overhangs the column; cards,
  tiles and fields darken their border (`--rule-hover`).

## Structure
Every way to move exists once, where a reader expects it:
- Bar: the name (home), Index (every framework by family, and each family's
  "How to choose"), search (⌘K, Ctrl+K or "/"), theme.
- Home (`#/`): intro + search + "Start from your situation", then the six
  families as a typographic index.
- Family (`#/category/:id`): the family's four frameworks, each with the
  question it answers, and one "How to choose" link.
- Guide (`#/framework/:slug[/:section]`, `#/compare/:slug[/:section]`): read
  one section at a time. The introduction (title, question, origin, lede,
  hero figure, "In short") and each chapter are panels. The contents rail
  (titled with the guide's name) switches them and ends with the family and
  "How to choose"; on narrow screens it is a sticky bar naming the guide and
  the section, and the introduction lists the sections. Each panel ends with
  previous/next; the last adds "Where next" (the rest of the family, then
  related frameworks from other families, each with a line on how they
  connect). No breadcrumb, no eyebrow labels: the rail already says where
  you are. Print shows the whole guide.
- Search with nothing typed lists the sections of the guide on screen.

## Content
Every guide is a canonical fragment in `public/guides/` written to
`docs/content-spec.md` and checked by `node scripts/check-guide.mjs --all`.
The app supplies all chrome and shared behaviour (quiz, copy, links, results).
Guides contain only content components and, where needed, a scoped widget.

## Principles
1. Readability is the product.
2. Calm chrome, rich content: colour and craft go into the figures and numbers.
3. One system: no page restyles a component; widgets use tokens only.
4. Never distort the teaching: every concept, example and tool survives.
5. No AI tells in visuals or copy: no emoji, no hype, no exclamation marks,
   no em-dash chains, sentence-case headings that say something.
